The Internet is not a single computer, a satellite or a giant server. It is a global network made up of smaller interconnected networks.


Copper cables — electrical signals.
Fiber-optic cables — pulses of light.
Wi-Fi — radio waves.

**************************************************************
Create a human Internet
Objective: Understand what a network of networks means.

Choose six students from the audience. Divide them into two groups, with one student from each group acting as a router.

Demonstration:
- Student 1 writes a message on a piece of paper.
- The message is passed to Router A.
- Router A passes it to Router B.
- Router B delivers it to Student 3.
- Disconnect the two routers and try again.
Conclusion: The Internet is simply many networks connected together. Each network can operate independently, but their connections allow them to communicate.

Send a message using a flashlight
Objective: Understand how binary data can be transmitted physically.

Use your phone's flashlight and establish two rules:
- Light ON = 1
- Light OFF = 0
Use eight equal time intervals to transmit the letter A, represented by 01000001. Ask a student to record each bit.

Conclusion: Fiber-optic communication uses a similar principle, encoding information in light signals, but at incredibly high speeds and with more sophisticated encoding.
